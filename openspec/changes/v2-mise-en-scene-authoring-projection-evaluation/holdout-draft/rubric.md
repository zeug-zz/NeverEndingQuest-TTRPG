# Holdout Artifact Rating Rubric (Draft)

**DRAFT / UNFROZEN / NOT OWNER-APPROVED.** This rubric is for future owner review only. No artifacts have been scored, and this document does not authorize scoring or evaluation.

## Rating unit and evidence

- Rate each exact scene-condition artifact version independently: six scenes x four conditions = 24 artifacts.
- Bind every rating to the scene ID, condition (`A`, `B`, `C`, or `D`), exact artifact SHA-256, axis, rater identity, and rating timestamp in any future approved record.
- Rate only the artifact shown. Do not infer omitted information from the source module, another condition, private/GM material, or prior ratings.
- Record a short rationale citing visible artifact content. Do not alter the artifact or resolve unknowns while rating it.
- The two axes are separate, integer ratings from 1 through 5. Owner ratings are authoritative; no assistant or advisory score substitutes for an owner rating.

## Legibility (1-5)

Judge how readily a reader can reconstruct the staged scene relationships and distinguish known facts, authored staging, and unknowns without contradiction.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The scene is not reconstructable; key relations are missing, contradictory, or materially misleading. |
| 2 | Only fragments are legible; important approach, threshold, focal, extent, identity, or knowledge boundaries are ambiguous. |
| 3 | The main scene is recoverable, but at least one important relation or distinction takes effort or remains unclear. |
| 4 | The approach, threshold, focal point, relevant extents, identities, and epistemic boundaries are readily understood with no material contradiction. |
| 5 | The complete relevant stage is immediately clear, coherent, and economical; source facts, Builder-authored decisions, and unknowns are unmistakably distinguished. |

## Usefulness (1-5)

Judge whether the artifact helps a Narrator or reader present an actionable, coherent arrival while preserving player agency and protected unknowns.

| Score | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | It provides no usable staging or encourages a critical false claim, leak, or unauthorized outcome. |
| 2 | It offers little practical help; an arrival or first interaction would require substantial reconstruction or guesswork. |
| 3 | It supports some useful orientation or action but leaves a notable staging gap or uncertainty about what is actionable. |
| 4 | It supports a coherent arrival and visible invitations to act, preserves unknowns, and does not prescribe a choice or success. |
| 5 | It makes the scene especially easy to present and act within, with vivid but bounded staging, coherent transitions, and clear agency-preserving invitations. |

## Critical-failure override and reporting

Any critical failure in the prospective protocol is recorded separately and cannot be averaged away: public guarded-content leakage or false activation; a protected unknown asserted as known; topology/route contradiction; an actionable prose-only fact; a relocated fixed anchor; a fabricated metric, compass bearing, sensory gradient, or mechanic; an access/door/legality inference from schematic placement; runtime-PC contamination; or a prescribed player choice/success. A critical violation fails the relevant future condition/party-shape cell regardless of ratings.

If separately approved, report each condition and party shape separately. The design's candidate threshold is mean legibility >= 4.0/5 and mean usefulness >= 4.0/5 in every condition; it is proposed, not ratified. Do not pool conditions, party shapes, scenes, development examples, or axes. Missing required artifact or rating evidence is BLOCKED, not a pass. A non-applicable assertion is excluded with a reason rather than counted as success.

This is an offline draft. It contains no scores, no owner decision, no assignment order, and no production-format recommendation. A fixture result does not demonstrate that an LLM Builder can stage a scene.
