# Night of the Restless Dead

## Introduction

### Module Overview
In this shadowed rural tale by Kuhal, the party begins at Ma's Watering Hole, a lonely inn between Yatar and Waterdeep, where rumor, road-dust, and unease gather beneath a single warm roof. From there the adventure leads into a ruined cathedral choked in webs and old sorrow, where hidden cult work festers in the dark and a handful of uneasy allies may offer respite, counsel, or grim necessity. The module contains 6 NPCs, 8 plot points, 1 location, and 7 creature stat blocks, all woven through a compact but atmospheric mystery of creeping corruption, desperate refuge, and a final confrontation with necromantic ambition.

### The Story So Far
The party's journey begins on the road at Ma's Watering Hole, where the ordinary comforts of ale and fire stand in stark contrast to the whispers of trouble gathering beyond the village. The innkeeper Ma keeps a watchful eye on travelers, while Blarg, her broad half-orc guardian, lends muscle to the threshold; from this humble haven, the party learns of a desecrated cathedral and the foul stirrings within. What first seems like a local haunting soon reveals itself as something more deliberate: a webbed ruin hiding signs of blood, abandonment, and ritual intent.

As the party presses into the cathedral, the air grows stale with decay and old prayers turned hollow. The main hall lies broken beneath thick webs, the storage rooms bear the stains of violence, and the priest's lodging opens onto a concealed descent where faint light leaks from below like a wound that refuses to close. In the lower passages, the true heart of the blight waits in a dead-end ritual chamber, where cultists and their allies scheme over experiments and dark power, driven by the promise of a necromantic ring. If the alarm is raised, the corridors become a trap; if the party survives the descent, they may retreat to Brother Lintar's sheltered refuge on the village outskirts to recover, present Saint Rydal's relic, and gather themselves for a renewed push into the cathedral's depths. When the immediate threat is broken, the road leads back to Ma's Watering Hole, where relief, lodging, and reward await--though the wider shadow of cult activity may still linger beyond the firelight.

### Running the Adventure
This adventure is written for a party of 3rd--5th level characters and works best with a standard group of four to five adventurers, though it can be adjusted for smaller or larger tables by trimming or reinforcing encounters. Before play, the DM should review the cathedral's layout, the cult's motivations, and the roles of the key NPCs so the investigation and confrontation feel connected rather than episodic. A few evocative descriptions of web-choked stone, candlelit ruin, and uneasy sanctuary will go a long way toward making this compact adventure feel like a living, haunted corner of the world.

# Plot Overview

Between the rutted road to Waterdeep and the wind-scoured fields of Yatar stands Ma's Watering Hole, a humble inn where wary travelers trade rumors over warm ale while Ma keeps a sharp eye and Blarg the half-orc guards the door like a living wall. But beyond this fragile haven waits a far darker place: a ruined cathedral swallowed by webs, shadow, and the stink of old blood, where a sheltered refuge on the village outskirts offers only brief respite before the party must press onward with Saint Rydal's relic in hand. Within the broken halls, something has been left hanging, something has been hidden below, and somewhere beneath the crumbling stone a cult whispers over forbidden experiments, building a necromantic horror in the dark. To uncover the truth, the heroes must descend into a place of collapsed tunnels, faintly lit doors, and waiting ambushes--where every step draws them closer to the heart of a blasphemous mystery that should have stayed buried.

# Locations

## NIG001 -- Night of the Restless Dead Main Area

Between Yatar and Waterdeep, Ma's Watering Hole squats like a stubborn lantern in the dark, its warm windows a defiant little promise against the miles of cold road and grave-haunted moor. The inn feels rural, practical, and lived-in: damp cloaks by the hearth, the smell of ale and stew, mud on the boots of travelers who have seen too much weather and too little mercy. Ma Thornfield keeps the place with a sharp eye and a sharper tongue, while Blarg, her hulking half-orc enforcer, lends the inn the sort of security that discourages bandits, drunks, and anything else that goes bump in the night. Nearby, Brother Lintar's modest stone refuge stands as a sacred counterpoint to the inn's rough comfort, a place of clean cots, bread, and barred doors where frightened souls can gather their courage before the road leads onward. Together, these places create a threshold atmosphere: one foot in civilization, one foot in the grave.

This area serves as the adventure's opening net, drawing the party from mundane shelter into a creeping web of corruption. The rooms and hidden passages connect the plot like a tightening noose: the inn offers the first clues and a secret route, the chapel provides safety and warning, and the ruined cathedral becomes the obvious place where the dead should not be stirring. Beneath the cathedral, the spaces narrow and darken, guiding the party from desecrated storage and buried living quarters into the hidden chamber where the true conspiracy is revealed. The layout reinforces the story's escalation--what begins as a roadside disturbance becomes evidence of organized necromancy, with each connected space pushing the party deeper toward the source of the unrest and the larger cult activity beyond.

The inhabitants reflect that descent. Ma and Brother Lintar are the human heart of the area, ordinary folk trying to hold back an extraordinary horror, while Father Aldric lingers as a figure tied to the cathedral's forgotten past and current secrets. Blarg stands as a blunt but loyal bulwark against the night, and the real threat gathers below in the form of Sister Miriam Bramble and Red, the Crimson Binder, whose presence marks the work of a practiced cult rather than a simple haunting.
